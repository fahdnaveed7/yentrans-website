India to USA

Ocean freight from India, door delivery in the USA

One contract from the Indian port to your consignee's facility.

The whole route, one file

The move starts at your plant. We place the equipment, supervise stuffing, file the export declaration and hand the container to the vessel at Cochin or whichever port suits the sailing. Our bill of lading covers the water leg, so there is no gap in liability at transhipment.

Before arrival in the United States we file the advance security data and the entry, settle duty and get the release. The box then leaves the terminal on our instruction and is trucked to the address on the delivery order, inside the receiving window the consignee has been given. One coordinator in Kochi holds the file from booking to delivery and tells you where the cargo is.

  • One bill of lading from the Indian port to the US door.
  • Customs clearance in India and the USA by one company.
  • Loading from Cochin and eight other Indian ports.
  • A named coordinator in Kochi on every shipment.

How we work

Booking to delivery

Rate and booking

We quote the sailing, the equipment and the inland leg together. Once you accept, space is booked and the cut-off dates go to your shipping desk.

Loading and export clearance

Equipment reaches your plant, stuffing is supervised, and the export declaration is filed. The container is gated in ahead of the cut-off and the bill of lading issued.

Ocean leg

The cargo sails on our bill of lading. We watch the transhipment port and call you if a connection slips, instead of waiting for you to ask.

US clearance and delivery

Entry is filed before the vessel berths. The container leaves the terminal on release, and a truck takes it to the consignee's facility at the agreed hour.

Industries

Where the cargo comes from

Different industries break in different places: temperature, documentation, delivery windows, duty exposure. These are the sectors we load from India most often.

Food and Beverage

Shelf life decides the routing, and the buyer's retail dates decide the sailing, not the freight rate.

Seafood and Aquaculture

Set points, pre-trip reports and US import filings that stand up to inspection at the first port.

Pharmaceuticals and Healthcare

Controlled temperature ranges, unbroken custody records, and no improvised handling when a vessel runs late.

Automotive and Components

Production lines do not wait, so transit reliability matters more than the cheapest available sailing.

Chemicals and Polymers

Correct classification, hazard paperwork and declarations that match the tariff line the US entry will use.

Textiles and Apparel

Season windows, high piece counts and buyers who deduct for a late delivery appointment.

Building Materials

Heavy, dense loads where weight limits, lashing and inland trucking costs decide the landed price.

Agriculture and Commodities

Volumes move against a contract date, so booking discipline and fumigation papers come first.

Industrial Equipment and Machinery

Out-of-gauge and breakbulk pieces need survey, lashing plans and a delivery site that can unload.
