### Two documents can describe one shipment at different levels

The bill of lading is central to ocean shipping, but the terms House Bill of Lading (HBL) and Master Bill of Lading (MBL) are often used as though they were interchangeable. They are not.

A Master Bill of Lading is generally issued by the ocean carrier to the party booking carriage with that carrier, often the NVOCC or freight forwarder in a consolidated arrangement. A House Bill of Lading is generally issued by an NVOCC or forwarding entity, where authorised, to its customer under the relevant contractual structure.

The party issuing a bill matters because it helps identify the parties to the contract of carriage and the terms that apply. A customer may see an HBL while a carrier issues an MBL for the same physical container movement. Names, references and shipment details need to be reconciled carefully across the documentation chain.

Why might this matter to an exporter or importer? Banks may have specific documentary instructions in letters of credit. Consignees and customs authorities may require particular information. Destination release procedures and contractual responsibilities also depend on how the shipment is structured.

A practical discussion before booking should cover whether a house bill will be used, who issues each document, the named shipper and consignee, any documentary credit requirements, the notify party, and the destination release procedure.

These choices should not be improvised after the vessel sails. Once documents enter a banking or customs process, even a minor inconsistency may create delays or additional costs.
