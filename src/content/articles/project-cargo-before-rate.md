### A project shipment begins with technical information

An ordinary carton can often be described by quantity, dimensions and gross weight. An oversized machine, industrial component or large piece of equipment needs a more detailed conversation.

The first requirement is accurate technical data. This may include a dimensional drawing, packed gross weight, lifting points, centre of gravity, packaging method and photographs. If the cargo consists of multiple pieces, each item needs its own specification.

Then comes the movement plan. Can the cargo travel on a standard road trailer? Are there bridge, turning-radius, height or axle-load restrictions? Does the port have suitable lifting equipment? Does the shipment require a flat rack, open-top container, breakbulk solution or other specialised arrangement?

Lifting and securing cannot be an afterthought. The parties responsible for packing, lifting, survey and cargo securing should be identified early, alongside any requirements for permits or approvals. Insurance and contractual responsibilities should reflect the actual movement.

A transport offer prepared without reliable technical information is often only a preliminary estimate. Changes in weight, dimensions or equipment can change the entire route and price.

For shippers, the best first step is to gather the drawings and practical site details before asking for a freight rate. That makes it easier for logistics teams and specialist partners to assess feasible options and their limitations.

**A realistic project cargo plan is built from the physical facts of the shipment.**
