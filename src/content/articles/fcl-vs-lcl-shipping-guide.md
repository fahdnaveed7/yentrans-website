### The right answer is not always the cheapest line item

FCL, or Full Container Load, is a movement in which the shipment uses a container allocated to a particular booking. LCL, or Less than Container Load, involves cargo consolidated with other consignments. Both can be effective, but they solve different planning problems.

A shipper may start by comparing freight prices. That makes sense, but the cost comparison should include more than the ocean segment. LCL movements can involve origin and destination consolidation charges, cargo handling, documentation and delivery arrangements. FCL movements can bring container-related costs, inland equipment needs and port or detention considerations. The exact charges vary by trade lane and provider.

Handling and timing also deserve attention. A consolidated shipment may depend on cutoff dates, warehouse receiving, loading and deconsolidation processes. A full-container shipment has its own container pickup and return requirements but can reduce some intermediate handling steps.

Product characteristics can change the decision. Fragile items, cargo requiring careful isolation, non-standard packaging or high-volume repeat shipments may benefit from a dedicated container. Smaller, less time-sensitive consignments may be better suited to consolidation.

It is also worth discussing the trade-off between a full container that is not completely utilised and an LCL movement that carries extra handling and destination charges. The answer depends on the lane, dimensions, weight, volume and delivery requirement.

When asking for a quote, share the cargo's packed dimensions, gross weight, commodity, origin, destination and ready date. A useful comparison should state what is included, what is excluded and what happens at the destination.

**The best choice is the one that fits the cargo and the total movement, not merely the smallest number beside "ocean freight."**
